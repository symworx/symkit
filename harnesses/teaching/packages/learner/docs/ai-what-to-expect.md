# AI in this course — what to expect

Short guide for **students** in this course. Your published **syllabus and LMS** win if anything here differs for a specific term.

## Preamble

**AI** is part of professional life in data and analytics. Policy will keep changing; the skill of **using AI as a tool while owning your work** will not go away.

Teaching full **agentic engineering** as a product skill is **not** the default objective of every course—unless your syllabus says otherwise. Learning **how to leverage AI without outsourcing understanding** is.

## What AI is for here

You may use AI tools to **support learning**, for example:

- Explaining a concept in different words  
- Helping debug **your** code or notebook (with sensitive data removed)  
- Brainstorming approaches or study questions  
- Checking your work against a rubric **after** you have attempted it  

You may use AI tools to **support productivity**, for example:

- Scaffolding a document or notebook **layout** (consider saving your initial prompt if the course asks)  
- Cleaning wording after *your* analysis is done  
- Generating practice problems for yourself  

AI is **not** a substitute for understanding the material, methods, or ethical constraints of the assignment.

## What remains your responsibility

- **You own everything submitted** and must be able to defend decisions if asked.  
- Graded work should reflect **your** reasoning unless an assignment explicitly allows more.  
- If you cannot explain or re-create what you submitted, you are not ready to turn it in.  
- You are responsible for errors, unsafe recommendations, and policy violations—even if a tool suggested them.  

## Allowed vs careful vs not OK (defaults)

| Usually OK | Be careful | Not OK (defaults) |
|:-----------|:-----------|:------------------|
| Concept questions, definitions | Large paste of your draft for “rewrite it all” | Submitting AI-generated work as solely yours when the goal is *your* skill |
| Explaining an error message | Using AI on data that is not clearly public | Putting **restricted** data in consumer AI tools |
| Practice problems you invent | Relying on AI for literature claims without checking sources | Uploading classmates’ work, answer keys, or private materials |
| Rubric self-check after your attempt | — | Ignoring a stricter rule on *this* course’s syllabus |

Individual courses may tighten or loosen this table. Always read the syllabus for your section.

## Data, privacy, and clinical / credentialed sources

See also [data-and-privacy.md](data-and-privacy.md).

- Do **not** paste PHI, real patient data, or **credentialed** extracts (e.g. MIMIC under a DUA) into consumer AI tools unless the instructor has named an **approved** path.  
- Prefer **public sample files** from the course repo (e.g. `data/public/`).  
- Credentials and API keys never go into chats.  

## Honesty and disclosure

See [disclosure.md](disclosure.md).

- If the syllabus or assignment asks you to **disclose** AI use, do so briefly (tool + purpose).  
- Fabricating sources, results, or “I ran this” when you did not is an integrity issue—with or without AI.  

## Workflow

Prefer the loop in [workflow.md](workflow.md): **attempt → assist → verify → own**.

## When policies conflict

1. **Syllabus / LMS for your term**  
2. This document and the rest of this kit  
3. Informal advice from tools or classmates  

## Questions

Ask the instructor or TA early—especially about data and disclosure—before a deadline makes the answer urgent.
