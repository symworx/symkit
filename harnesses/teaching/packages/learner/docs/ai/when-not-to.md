# When not to use AI (or use extreme caution)

Defaults for this course. Your syllabus may be stricter.

## Do not

| Situation | Why |
|:--|:--|
| **Restricted / clinical data** in consumer tools | DUA, HIPAA, privacy, and trust |
| **Credentials, keys, tokens** in chats | Security |
| **Classmates’ solutions** or answer keys into a model | Integrity + often not yours to share |
| **Fabricating** citations, results, or “I ran it” | Academic integrity |
| **Full submission generation** when the goal is skill demonstration | You can’t defend it |
| **Bypassing** a “no AI” or limited-AI assignment | Follow the assignment |

## Be careful

| Situation | Safer approach |
|:--|:--|
| Large rewrite of your draft | Ask for gaps / rubric check, then rewrite yourself |
| Literature claims | Verify every source yourself |
| Autocomplete in IDEs | Review every line you keep |
| Translating or polishing non-native writing | Allowed in many courses if the **ideas and analysis are yours**—confirm locally |
| Peer review of others’ work | Don’t upload their full submission to a public model without guidance |

## “I only used it a little”

If the graded intellectual work (method choice, analysis, interpretation, design) came from the tool, it is not “a little.” If it fixed a typo or explained an error you then applied, that is usually fine under org defaults.

## Gray areas → ask

- Whether a dataset is public enough for external tools  
- Whether a take-home exam allows AI  
- Whether group work may use shared AI chats  

Ask **before** the deadline.
